The important thing about games like Hide a Base and Steal isn't that they have lots of systems. It's that the player immediately understands:

Do thing → get money → make base better → get better stuff → do thing again.

Your current loop sounds more like:

Rob → get money → buy cosmetics/house upgrades → rob again.

The missing piece is a repeatable active money-making activity.

I'd make the "one more thing" a Piggy Job system
Instead of introducing a complicated quest system, give players 3–5 tiny jobs that appear around the map.

For example:

🐷 Feed the Piggies — find 3 food bowls and fill them.

💰 Piggy Bank Delivery — pick up a piggy bank and deliver it to an NPC.

🔨 Fix the Bank — hit 5 broken spots around an NPC's house.

🚚 Piggy Bank Truck — follow a truck and grab dropped cash.

🕵️ Catch the Thief — an NPC piggy gets robbed; chase the thief and recover the money.

🧹 Clean the Pig Pen — clean 5 messes scattered around the map.

🎁 Lost Piggy Bank — find a hidden bank somewhere in the world.

The key is that these should take ~30–90 seconds, not 5 minutes.

And they should pay real money, not just XP.

The loop becomes
Rob players/NPCs
↓
Do a quick job
↓
Earn cash + maybe a crate
↓
Upgrade house / buy dog / unlock cosmetics
↓
Go back out into the world
↓
Rob someone carrying valuable loot

That is already a much stronger game.

But I'd add one particularly important mechanic
Make players physically carry valuable things home.
This is what I think you're missing from the Hide a Base and Steal comparison.

The fun isn't necessarily "pets generate $/sec."

It's:

I found something valuable → now I have to successfully get it home.

That creates tension.

You could have Piggy Jobs generate loot crates rather than immediately giving cash.

For example:

Small Job

🐷 Feed 3 pigs
Reward: Common Crate

Medium Job

🏦 Deliver a stolen piggy bank
Reward: Rare Crate

Hard Job

🚨 Recover the Golden Piggy Bank
Reward: Epic Crate

Then the player has to physically carry the crate back to their house.

And here's where your existing robbery system becomes much more interesting:

Other players can steal your crate.
Now you have:

Job → loot → transport → defend → deposit → reward

That's an actual gameplay loop rather than just another menu.

Don't make the crate system complicated
I'd probably make the crate contain one of three things:

🐷 Piggy Skin
Common → Uncommon → Rare → Epic → Legendary → Mythic

🏠 House item
Furniture, decorations, walls, doors, etc.

🐕 Dog item
Dog skins/variations, collars, accessories, etc.

You already have these cosmetic categories, so you don't need to invent another progression system.

The crate system simply becomes the mechanism for obtaining them.

This also solves your "why am I robbing?" problem
Right now, robbing sounds like the activity itself is the reward.

Instead:

Robbing is how you acquire valuable loot.

And then:

Jobs are another way to acquire valuable loot.

And potentially:

Other players can steal that loot from you before you get home.

Now the game has a reason to leave your house.

I'd actually avoid making "passive income" the main progression
Your comparison game can get away with pets generating money because stealing pets is itself the active gameplay.

Your game already has an active equivalent:

stealing piggy banks.

So you don't necessarily need to copy the pets-$-per-second system.

I'd make the economy something like:

Robbing

Fast

Risky

High reward

Jobs

Reliable

Active

Medium reward

House

Permanent progression

Crates

Exciting rewards

Piggy skins

Collection/completion

Dogs

Collection + perhaps tiny gameplay utility

This gives different types of players something to do without turning the game into an RPG.

One thing I'd seriously consider: a "wanted" system
This could make your robbery loop substantially more entertaining without adding much complexity.

Every time you successfully rob someone, you accumulate Heat.

For example:

🔥 HEAT: 32%

At high Heat:

police NPCs start appearing

security dogs chase you

your location gets revealed periodically

you drop more money if caught

but successful robberies give bigger rewards

So players have a choice:

"Do I keep robbing because I'm on a hot streak, or go home and bank my money?"

That's a very simple risk/reward decision.

And importantly, it creates emergent player stories.

"I had $15,000 on me and three cops were chasing me and I barely made it home."

That's the sort of thing kids remember.

Your game should probably have only ~4 major things
If you're worried about becoming too complicated, I'd actually establish a hard rule:

The player should understand the entire game in 30 seconds.
Something like:

1. Rob stuff.
Steal piggy banks from NPCs and players.

2. Do jobs.
Complete little activities around the map for crates/cash.

3. Get the loot home.
Other players can steal valuable loot you're carrying.

4. Upgrade your house and collection.
Spend your money and collect better piggies, dogs and decorations.

That's enough.

You don't need crafting.

You don't need skill trees.

You don't need 15 currencies.

You don't need elaborate quests.

You don't need complicated stats.

And I wouldn't underestimate the cosmetics
You said:

"they only gain cosmetics from this"

But that's not necessarily a problem.

For a Roblox game aimed at younger players, collecting itself can be the progression.

The important question isn't:

"Does this cosmetic have gameplay value?"

It's:

"Do I really want the next one?"

If the answer is yes, you've created a progression system.

Think about how satisfying this could be:

Piggy Collection

Common: 12/30
Uncommon: 8/25
Rare: 4/20
Epic: 2/15
Legendary: 0/10
Mythic: 0/5

Then you have:

🔒 GOLDEN PIGGY — 0/1

And the player thinks:

"I need that."

That's enough.

One particularly strong idea: Piggy Museum
This could give all those skins a purpose without changing your core gameplay.

Players have a Piggy Museum/Collection Room inside their house.

Every piggy they obtain gets displayed.

Completing collections gives small rewards.

For example:

Farm Collection
🐷 Farmer Piggy
🐷 Tractor Piggy
🐷 Cowboy Piggy
🐷 Chicken Piggy
🐷 Golden Farmer Piggy

5/5 COMPLETE → Farm Trophy

Then:

Space Collection
🚀 Astronaut Piggy
👽 Alien Piggy
🌙 Moon Piggy
⭐ Galaxy Piggy
🌌 Cosmic Piggy

5/5 COMPLETE → Space House Theme

Now cosmetics aren't just "skins in a menu."

They're things you're hunting for and displaying.

The biggest mistake I'd avoid
Don't respond to the stale feeling by adding more content.

If you currently have:

Robbing → house → skins → dogs

and you're worried it's stale, the solution isn't:

Robbing → quests → pets → crafting → achievements → battles → trading → rebirths → talents → collections → events...

You'll end up with a game that has 30 systems but no clear identity.

Instead, I'd add one verb:

"Complete jobs and bring the loot home."

Then make your existing systems reinforce that.

If I were prototyping this tomorrow
I'd make one tiny vertical slice:

🐷 JOB BOARD
Every 2 minutes, 3 jobs appear:

Feed 3 Piggies

$500 + Common Crate

Deliver a Piggy Bank

$1,500 + Uncommon Crate

Recover the Golden Piggy Bank

$5,000 + Rare Crate

Player chooses one.

They go do it.

They receive a physical crate.

CRATE ACQUIRED!

They have to carry it home.

Other players can see:

🔴 PLAYERNAME HAS A RARE CRATE!

Now there's an opportunity to intercept them.

They make it home.

OPEN CRATE

🎉 RARE COWBOY PIGGY

Put it in their collection.

Then they're immediately thinking:

"Okay, how do I get the next one?"

That's the loop I'd test before building anything else.
