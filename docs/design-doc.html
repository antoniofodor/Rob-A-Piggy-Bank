The hook

Why this and not another tycoon

The tycoon shelf on Roblox is full. What's thin is a game where the thing you're hoarding is physically visible and physically takeable. Your coins aren't a number in the corner of the screen — they're a pile inside a glass vault on your plot, and the pile gets taller as you get richer. Anyone in the server can see how rich you are from across the map. That's the whole pitch, and a nine-year-old gets it in one look.

It works for this age group because it turns a solo idle game into a story they tell at school. Nobody recounts the time their crops grew. They absolutely recount the time Jayden snuck onto their plot, and the guard dog got him halfway home.

The design pillar everything hangs off

Every coin you spend goes to defense or offense — never both. Within a week players self-sort into turtles and bandits, argue about which is better, and have a reason to keep playing that isn't a bigger number. That split is the game.

Core loop

Ten seconds to understand, no tutorial

Solid path: the idle loop. Dashed path: the reason anyone stays.

The idle loop alone is a competent, forgettable game. The dashed path is what makes it spread — getting robbed creates a grievance, and a grievance creates a session. The revenge ping is the single highest-value piece of UI in the whole build.

The 8-hour offline cap is deliberate: it covers a school day, so a kid who plays after school always comes back to a full vault. Longer caps make the game feel pointless to check daily; shorter ones punish having a life.

The heist

The thirty seconds the game is actually about

Plots have no walls you can't cross. Anyone can walk onto anyone's land at any time — the tension comes from what happens next, not from being locked out.

  1. Approach. Crossing a defended plot is slow and noisy. Fences cost you seconds, alarms light you up in red for everyone to see.
  2. The grab. Hold E on the vault for 3 seconds. You're stuck in place, glowing, and the whole server can see it. Longer as the owner buys better locks.
  3. The run. You get a visible sack worth 5% of their vault. You're 25% slower carrying it, and you have to physically reach your own plot to bank it.
  4. The tag. Anyone — the owner, a bystander, a guard dog — can hit you with a pie. You drop the sack, coins fly back to the owner, and you're stunned for 4 seconds.

Everything in that sequence is legible from a distance without reading a word. A kid who joins mid-chase understands what's happening instantly, which is what makes it contagious.

Design note

The 3-second hold matters more than any other number here. Instant stealing is a drive-by with no drama. Ten seconds and nobody ever succeeds. Three seconds is long enough to get caught and short enough to be worth trying.

Two ways to spend

The choice that makes players into characters

Defense

The turtle

  • Fences — five tiers, each slows crossings further
  • Alarm — highlights the thief, pings you wherever you are
  • Guard dog — auto-chases and tags, 30s cooldown
  • Vault locks — grab time 3s → 5s → 8s
  • Floodlights — kills the smoke bomb's cover

Offense

The bandit

  • Speed boots — cancels the carry slowdown
  • Lockpicks — five tiers, each cuts grab time
  • Bigger sack — 5% → 12% per grab
  • Smoke bomb — 6s invisible to dogs and alarms
  • Bolt cutters — walk through fences at full speed

Coins spent on one tree are gone from the other. That scarcity is the point — it forces an identity, and identity is what kids argue about, post about, and come back to defend. A rebirth is the only way to respec, which quietly makes rebirthing feel like a fresh start rather than a reset.

Keeping it fair

The rules that stop this becoming a bullying simulator

A steal mechanic aimed at under-12s fails in one specific way: a strong player parks on a weak player's plot and farms them until they quit. Every rule below exists to make that impossible rather than merely discouraged.

RuleValueWhat it prevents
Per-grab cap5% of vaultNo single raid ever feels devastating
Per-victim cooldown60 secRapid-fire farming of one player
Grabs per victim3 per sessionCamping one plot all game
Offline owner cap2.5%Being away from keyboard as a liability
New player shieldFirst 15 minGetting robbed before you know the controls
Banked coinsUntouchableAnything spent on upgrades is permanently safe

That last row does the heaviest lifting. Only uncollected coins are ever stealable, so the game teaches a real lesson — bank often — instead of punishing you for logging off. A player who gets robbed loses a few minutes of idle income, never progress.

Worth stating plainly

Reward coming back; never punish leaving. Timers built around loss (your crops wilt, your streak dies) do retain players, but they retain them through anxiety — and on a game aimed at nine-year-olds that's both the wrong thing to build and the kind of thing that draws moderation attention. A full vault waiting for you performs about as well and ages a great deal better.

The long game

What keeps week three interesting

Rebirth

At one million coins you can rebirth: upgrades reset, you gain a permanent +25% income multiplier, and your vault changes material — wood, then iron, then gold, then diamond. The material is visible from across the map, so your rebirth count is public status without a single number on screen. Kids will grind for the diamond vault specifically because everyone can see it.

Social pressure, built in

  • Server leaderboard — richest vault, updated live, visible on a board at spawn
  • Revenge ping — “Jayden robbed you!” with a button that marks their plot. Highest-value UI in the game
  • Friend bonus — +10% income per friend in the server, capped at +50%. This is your growth engine; it makes inviting people a mechanical advantage rather than a favor
  • Most wanted — the server's top thief gets a hat nobody can buy

Weekly drops

At 10–20 hours a week you can sustain a real cadence, and you have to — static games don't hold chart positions. Budget roughly: one new vault skin or cosmetic each week, one new upgrade tier or defense type each month, one themed event each season. Announce every drop in-game on a board at spawn so returning players immediately see there's something new.

Monetization

Built for the under-13 rules from day one

Roblox prohibits paid random-chance items for under-13 accounts, and that's your entire audience. Every purchase must be a known quantity — you pay, you get exactly the named thing. No crates, no spins, no mystery eggs. Designing to this now is much cheaper than retrofitting after a policy strike.

ItemTypePriceRationale
2× IncomeGamepass99 R$The reliable earner in every tycoon
Auto-CollectGamepass199 R$Convenience, not power — still capped by vault size
Big SackGamepass149 R$+3% per grab. Deliberately small so it isn't pay-to-win
Vault skinsGamepass49–99 R$Pure cosmetic, and highly visible — the best kind
Coin packsProductTieredFixed amounts, clearly labelled

Keep every paid advantage inside what a free player can reach through play. The moment a bandit can only be stopped by someone who paid, the free players — who are the overwhelming majority and the entire reason the servers feel alive — stop showing up.

Architecture

What I'll be building on your behalf

An economy where players take things from each other is the exact scenario exploiters go after. Two non-negotiables carry most of the weight:

Server-authoritative everything

The client renders coins; it never counts them. Every grab, collect, purchase and upgrade is validated server-side against the player's real state, with rate limits on every remote. A client that says “I collected nine trillion coins” gets ignored and flagged. This is not optional in a PvP economy, and it's the thing most solo Roblox games get wrong.

Session-locked saves

Profile-style datastore handling with session locking, so the same player joining two servers can't duplicate their vault — a classic Roblox dupe. Offline income is computed on join from a lastSave timestamp, clamped to 8 hours, never trusted from the client's clock.

SystemApproach
Plot assignmentPool of 12, claimed on join, released and wiped on leave
Coin pile visualSingle mesh scaled on a log curve — not hundreds of parts, which would tank framerate
Steal validationServer checks distance, cooldown, session count, and shield before granting
Data schemaVersioned from the first commit so live saves can be migrated later
Source controlRojo, so scripts live in git and I can work on them as real files

Build order

Sequenced so there's something playable early

1

Foundation

~1 week · nothing fun yet

Plot pool and assignment, coin accrual, the visible pile, save and load with session locking, offline accrual on join.

Unglamorous and completely load-bearing. Getting the data layer right now avoids a rewrite in month two, when wiping saves means wiping real players.

2

Economy and shop

~1 week · first playable

Collect interaction, income and capacity upgrade tiers, shop UI, the first-minute pacing.

End of this phase it's a working idle game. Play it yourself for an evening — if the first five minutes are boring, no amount of stealing later will save it.

3

The heist

~1–2 weeks · the real test

Grab interaction, carry state, the run home, pie tagging, and every fairness rule from the table above wired in from the start.

This is where the game becomes itself. Get real kids on it as soon as it works — the 3-second hold and 5% cap will both need tuning against actual behavior, not theory.

4

Defense and offense trees

~1 week

Both upgrade trees, guard dog AI, alarms, smoke bombs, and the visual language that makes a defended plot readable at a glance.

5

Retention systems

~1 week

Rebirth and vault materials, leaderboards, revenge pings, friend bonus, most-wanted hat.

6

Polish and launch

~1 week · do not skip

Sound, particles, screen shake on a successful grab, coin pickup juice, gamepasses, analytics, thumbnail and icon.

Juice is not decoration on a game like this — the grab has to feel like a heist. Budget real time here. Your thumbnail also does more for discovery than any feature in phase 4.

7

Soft launch and live ops

Ongoing

Ship quietly, watch the numbers, tune the economy against real players, then start the weekly drop rhythm. Expect the first version's balance to be wrong — every one of these games launches wrong and fixes it live.

Open calls

Decisions I need from you, roughly in the order they'll block me

Is the title right?

“Rob a Piggy Bank” matches the naming convention that currently wins Roblox search — short, a verb, exactly what you do. The risk is that the “Steal a …” pattern is saturated enough to read as derivative. Worth deciding before art gets made.

Art direction: blocky or meshy?

Pure part-built is faster, cheaper, runs on school Chromebooks, and looks native to Roblox. Meshes look better in a thumbnail. For this audience and your hours, I'd lean blocky with strong colour and good lighting.

Does trading exist?

Trading massively deepens a collecting economy, but it drags in scam risk, moderation load, and a whole second exploit surface. My recommendation is no trading at launch, revisited once the core is stable.

How much do you want to build yourself?

I can write essentially all of it, but a game you don't understand is one you can't run live-ops on. Suggest I build the systems and you own layout, art, and balance tuning — that's also the half that's more fun.
